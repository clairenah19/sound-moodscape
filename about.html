Moodscape

소리로 느끼는 한국 — Feeling Korea Through Sound
A data sonification project exploring whether geographic and demographic data can be translated into music that communicates the character of a place.
📍
What's actually running vs. what's proposed. This page separates the parts of Moodscape that exist in the live app (the interactive map) from the research concept and roadmap items that are still design proposals. Badges on each section show the current status.

01 Research question

Can geographic and demographic data be converted into music that lets a listener understand the character of a place — without seeing a map?

Most geographic tools communicate visually: maps, charts, colour gradients. Moodscape investigates whether sound can carry the same information, and whether a piece of music can feel like a more emotionally accurate representation of a place than a number on a chart.

Hypothesis: a listener who hears a Moodscape sonification for an unfamiliar province, without being told which one it is, will correctly guess its relative population density, energy level, and safety at a rate above chance.

02 Why sonification

Sonification — turning data into non-speech audio — sits at the intersection of computer science, cognitive psychology, and data science. Geographic sonification specifically is under-explored, and it addresses a real accessibility gap: maps, heat maps, and choropleth charts are inaccessible to blind and low-vision users, a limitation documented by prior academic work such as the University of Maryland's iSonic project (ACM TOCHI, 2008) and a 2024 systematic review noting the lack of live, assessed sonification tools for this group. Instrumentation also carries cultural meaning a bare statistic can't: a gayageum playing a slow phrase signals "ancient and still" in a way the number 48 does not.

03 The sonification algorithm Pitch/tempo/timbre/drift mapping built — scoring formula not yet wired in

The research design behind Moodscape scores each province on a 0–100 "mood score" from a weighted formula. An earlier version of this document labelled the density term "population density, inverse" — that was backwards: the data shows the opposite relationship. Seoul has both the highest density (16,000/km²) and the highest mood score (92); Jeju has the lowest of both. Density scales the score up, toward "exciting," not down. The corrected formula:

Mood Score = (Safety × 0.30) + (English proficiency × 0.25) + (Density Score × 0.25) + (Annual Temp Score × 0.20)

That score drives four audio parameters, plus a fifth — Seasonal Drift — computed independently from the province's temperature swing rather than the composite score:

🎵 Pitch
A4 (440 Hz) at score 0 → A5 (880 Hz) at score 100, roughly 1–2 semitones per 10 points.
📊 Waveform height
Bars in the player grow with score — tall and dense for high-energy places, short and spacious for calm ones.
🥁 Tempo
40 BPM (meditative) at score 0 to 120 BPM (driving) at score 100, mapped linearly.
🎻 Timbre
0–35: traditional acoustic (gayageum, daegeum, haegeum). 36–65: ambient-electronic hybrid. 66–100: synth, electric bass, digital beats.
🍂 Seasonal Drift — new
Pitch/timbre arcs from a cooler "winter" register toward a brighter "summer" one and partway back over the piece's runtime, scaled by that province's actual annual temperature swing.

Density Score — named zones, not a raw inverse

Korean provincial density spans more than two orders of magnitude (≈50/km² in the emptiest counties to 16,000/km² in central Seoul), so a linear scale would flatten everything except Seoul to near-zero. Density Score uses a log curve anchored between a floor of 50/km² ("empty") and a ceiling of 18,000/km² (just above Seoul's cited peak, "overcrowded"):

Density Score = 100 × (log₁₀(density) − log₁₀(50)) / (log₁₀(18,000) − log₁₀(50)), clamped to [0, 100]

That continuous score is then labelled into six named zones, the same way the app already buckets mood scores into "Calm" / "Balanced" / "Lively":

0–15 · Empty
Remote countryside — near-zero foot traffic, silence dominates.
15–35 · Sparse
Rural provinces, small towns, farmland.
35–55 · Comfortable
Suburbs and small cities — room to breathe, still populated.
55–75 · Bustling
Urban districts, active streets, visible crowds.
75–90 · Dense
Major metro areas — constant activity, tight blocks.
90–100 · Overcrowded
Megacity core — maximum foot traffic, near-continuous crowding.

Applied to the four researched provinces (Jeju's density is derived here from its cited population ÷ its ≈1,833 km² land area, since no density figure for the province was directly cited earlier):

Seasonal Drift — the temperature swing, not just the average

The original formula reduced each province's climate to a single annual average, which erases Korea's four sharply distinct seasons. Every province already has cited January (winter) and August (summer) normals — their difference is the Seasonal Swing, and it drives a genuinely new audio dimension rather than folding into the static mood score:

Seasonal Swing = August avg temp − January avg temp Drift Depth = Seasonal Swing / 30°C (clamped to [0, 1], ~30°C ≈ the widest realistic Korean provincial swing)

A province with a wide swing gets a piece that audibly evolves — the pitch centre and timbre brightness ramp up through a simulated "spring→summer" arc across the first half of the 15 seconds, then ease back down through "autumn→winter" in the second half. A province with a narrow swing stays comparatively steady throughout. Spring and autumn aren't independently sourced here — they're modelled as the linear transition between the cited winter and summer normals, which is an honest simplification, not independent KMA seasonal data.

Seoul
−2.4°C → 25.7°C → swing 28.1°C → Drift Depth 0.94 (most dramatic arc)
Incheon
−2.9°C → 25.2°C → swing 28.1°C → Drift Depth 0.94
Busan
4.2°C → 27.3°C → swing 23.1°C → Drift Depth 0.77
Jeju-do
6.1°C → 28.0°C → swing 21.9°C → Drift Depth 0.73 (steadiest of the four)

In the current app, place and province "mood scores" are hand-assigned rather than computed live from this formula, and neither the corrected Density Score nor Seasonal Drift is wired into the audio yet.

Audio: real AI tracks (Suno), with a synth fallback

Each place resolves its soundscape through three layers, in order: (1) a pre-generated real track — a hosted URL, or an MP3 dropped into the audio/ folder (generated in Suno from a per-place prompt the app builds automatically); (2) fully automatic live generation if a paid sunoapi.org API key is saved in the browser — request/response shape verified against their published API docs, not guessed; (3) a built-in Web Audio synth fallback so the player is never silent. The prompts are deliberately genre-distinct by mood band — a "very peaceful" place asks Suno for slow ambient drone, a "very exciting" one for driving EDM — so tracks differ sharply from each other, not just in pitch. When a real track plays it's routed through a live audio analyser, so the waveform reacts to the actual audio. Suno itself has no official public API, which is why layer (2) depends on a paid third-party provider rather than Suno directly; whether that provider's server allows direct browser-origin requests (CORS) is unverified, so the download-and-drop workflow in layer (1) remains the dependable path if live generation fails silently against CORS.

05 Roadmap Proposed — not yet built

These five ideas are design proposals for where Moodscape could go next. None of them exist in the current app yet; they're listed here so the goals are documented and each can be checked off as it lands.

🌡️ Live weather-to-pitch

Pull today's actual temperature per province from the free Open-Meteo API and offset the baseline pitch/tempo from it, so the same province sounds different on a hot day than a cold one.

♿ Accessibility mode

A non-visual interface: auto-play a province's note on focus, screen-reader-friendly stat readouts, and stereo positioning so the map has a physical sense in audio space — aimed at blind and low-vision users.

🔬 User study

A structured listening test (sighted and blind/low-vision participants) to check whether people can correctly infer density, safety, and temperature from the sonification alone, above chance.

🤖 AI mood narrative generator

Generate a short, non-repeating text description of each province's soundscape from its live mood score, note, tempo, and instrumentation via a language model.

📦 Open dataset

Publish the mood-score formula, source statistics, and resulting musical parameters for all 17 South Korean provinces (not just the four studied so far) so others can reuse or extend it.

06 Related work

NASA — Black Hole Sonification (2022)

NASA's Chandra X-ray Centre mapped pressure waves from the Perseus galaxy cluster into audible sound by scaling them 57 octaves upward, with X-ray brightness as pitch and distance from the black hole as stereo position. Patterns invisible in the raw image became audible in seconds. Moodscape borrows the same basic move — spatial/geographic data mapped directly to audio parameters.

iSonic — Geographic Sonification for Blind Users (ACM TOCHI, 2008)

The University of Maryland's HCI Lab built an interactive sonification tool letting blind users explore georeferenced data through coordinated sound and speech; testing with blind participants showed they could identify facts and trends in unfamiliar geographic data by ear. It's the closest academic precedent for Moodscape's proposed accessibility mode.
